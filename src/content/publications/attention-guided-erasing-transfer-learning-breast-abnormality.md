  sections:
    - id: "problem"
      label: "01 / THE PROBLEM"
      title: "Random erasing can delete the evidence."
      duration: "20 sec"
      body:
        - "Medical imaging datasets are often too small for a model to learn robust task-specific representations from scratch. Transfer learning helps, but common augmentations such as random erasing are blind to anatomy: they can remove a lesion, dense tissue, or another class-relevant region just as easily as irrelevant background."
        - "The research question was whether a self-supervised model could provide a better erasing policy. DINO attention maps weakly localize salient mammographic structures without pixel-level labels, creating a way to retain likely evidence while still introducing useful training variation."
        - "That distinction matters because AGE is not trained as a lesion segmenter. The attention map supplies a weak, dataset-derived cue for augmentation, not a clinical contour or ground-truth explanation. The method has to improve downstream classification without quietly turning extra spatial annotations into an advantage."
    - id: "idea"
      label: "02 / THE IDEA"
      title: "Use attention as the erasing policy."
      duration: "45 sec"
      body:
        - "A Vision Transformer is first pretrained with DINO on the target dataset. The six final-layer attention heads are converted into binary masks. Instead of cutting out an arbitrary rectangle, AGE keeps the attended region and erases the surrounding background with a chosen probability during downstream training."
        - "Attention-head selection is task-specific: each candidate head is evaluated on validation data, and the head with the strongest validation F1 is used for final transfer learning. The mechanism therefore adapts to the visual evidence needed for density, malignancy, calcification, or mass classification."

        - "Every experiment compared no erasing, conventional random erasing, and AGE. Models were run five times, and the paper reports mean macro F1 with standard deviation to reduce the effect of class imbalance and run-to-run variability."
        - "The task range is part of the test. Whole mammograms contain large areas of background and broad tissue patterns, while cropped ROIs can be dominated by the abnormality itself. Evaluating both settings asks when a guided erasing policy has useful context to remove—and when almost every pixel may already matter."

    - id: "results"
      label: "04 / WHAT HAPPENED"
      title: "AGE helped when attention isolated the right evidence."
      duration: "55 sec"
      body:
        - "AGE produced the best mean macro F1 in every evaluated task, but the magnitude and certainty varied. Improvements were statistically significant for breast density, whole-image CEM malignancy, calcification ROIs, and CEM lesion ROIs."
        - "The mass ROI task exposed the limit: the relevant structure already occupied most of the patch, so there was little uninformative background to remove. AGE moved F1 only from 0.5530 to 0.5537 and was not statistically significant."
        - "The result is therefore conditional rather than universal. AGE is most convincing when attention can separate useful evidence from disposable context. The five-task comparison also prevents the headline result from hiding the weak case: the same mechanism that helps four tasks has almost no leverage when the crop is already tightly focused."

      title: "Attention reveals why the effect changes by task."
      duration: "55 sec"
      body:
        - "The selected heads focus on different structures: dense breast tissue for density assessment, enhancement patterns for CEM malignancy, and compact bright regions for calcifications. The qualitative chain links the original image to attention, binary mask, and final erased view."
        - "These maps are not lesion annotations or clinical explanations. They are weak localizations used to shape augmentation. Their value is visible when the salient region is smaller than the input; when the ROI fills the patch, as in the mass task, guided erasing has less room to help."
