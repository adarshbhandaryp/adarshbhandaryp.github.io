  sections:
    - id: "problem"
      label: "01 / THE PROBLEM"
      title: "Most breast-imaging AI stops before the report."
      duration: "20 sec"
      body:
        - "Many mammography models solve one isolated task—density estimation, lesion classification, or detection. A radiology report must combine several attributes into coherent language, so moving from a high-resolution image to structured text requires both visual representation learning and controlled language generation."
        - "Public mammography datasets also use different labels, conventions, and acquisition settings. Before a model can learn image-to-text alignment, those sources must be transformed into a consistent training corpus without pretending that heterogeneous labels are already interchangeable clinical reports."
        - "The challenge is therefore both architectural and curatorial. A generator cannot learn a stable mapping if identical findings are described through incompatible target formats, yet aggressive harmonization can erase meaningful source differences. MammoBLIP treats standardization as part of the modeling pipeline rather than an invisible preprocessing detail."
    - id: "idea"
      label: "02 / THE IDEA"
      title: "Align the image and the report before generating text."
      duration: "50 sec"
      body:
        - "MammoBLIP starts with a frozen EVA-CLIP Vision Transformer that converts each mammogram into patch features. A lightweight MammoBLIP transformer projects image and text representations into a shared 256-dimensional space, and a symmetric contrastive objective pulls matched pairs together."
        - "For generation, the visual embedding is inserted between a prompt and the target report tokens. Frozen BioMedLM then predicts the report autoregressively. Training updates only the multimodal transformer and projection heads, keeping both large backbones frozen to reduce the compute required for adaptation."
        - "The two objectives solve different parts of the problem. Contrastive learning gives paired images and reports a common representation, while autoregressive training teaches the language model to express the target sequence one token at a time. The trainable bridge carries the modality-specific adaptation between those frozen systems."

      title: "Five sources became one standardized corpus."
      duration: "35 sec"
      body:
        - "The study curated 81,076 images from VinDR-Mammo, RSNA, CMMD, InBreast, and KAU. Images were normalized through a shared preprocessing pipeline, while available labels and report information were converted into standardized text targets capped at 100 tokens."
        - "Stratified sampling produced 59,089 training pairs, 8,745 validation pairs, and 13,242 test pairs. Training ran for ten epochs on one NVIDIA Quadro RTX 8000, with the vision encoder and biomedical language model frozen."
        - "This design tests whether lightweight multimodal adaptation is feasible at multi-dataset scale. It does not make the five sources identical: the held-out results are still reported by dataset so that differences in sample count, acquisition, and target language remain visible rather than disappearing inside one pooled score."

    - id: "results"
      label: "04 / WHAT HAPPENED"
      title: "The reports matched targets lexically and semantically."
      duration: "50 sec"
      body:
        - "Across the held-out set, MammoBLIP reached overall BLEU 65.36, ROUGE-1 0.75, BERT-F1 0.88, and sentence-embedding similarity 0.91. These metrics measure complementary properties: token overlap, semantic correspondence, and sentence-level similarity."
        - "Performance varied by source. KAU produced the strongest reported scores, while InBreast was the most difficult, reflecting its smaller size and greater variation in acquisition and terminology. That spread matters: a single aggregate score can hide how strongly dataset standardization affects generation quality."
        - "Agreement metrics are useful for benchmarking, but they are not a substitute for clinical error analysis. A fluent report can remain wrong about a finding, omit an important attribute, or mirror dataset wording without demonstrating diagnostic reasoning. The reported scores establish image-to-text alignment; they do not establish safe autonomous reporting."
