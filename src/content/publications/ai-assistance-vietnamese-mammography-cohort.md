abstract: "Retrospective reader study of AI-assisted mammography screening in a Vietnamese cohort with a high prevalence of dense breast tissue. Reads with and without commercial AI support were compared on BI-RADS assessment and ROC-based sensitivity at Hanoi Medical University Hospital."
plainLanguageSummary: "Mammographic sensitivity drops in dense breasts, and dense tissue is common in Vietnamese screening populations. This retrospective study compared radiologist reads with and without commercial AI support on a Hanoi Medical University Hospital cohort collected between November 2022 and April 2023, from a base of 1,119 eligible patients. The AI-assisted read reported AUC 75.2% (95% CI 75.0–75.5) and sensitivity 61.5% (95% CI 60.8–61.6), against AUC 73.2% and sensitivity 57.5% for the unassisted read — roughly a four-point absolute sensitivity gain. It is a retrospective e-poster study, not a prospective trial."
contribution: "Provides cohort-level evidence on whether AI assistance changes screening sensitivity in a population where breast density is a systematic limitation, rather than on a curated benchmark."
method: "Retrospective analysis of a Hanoi Medical University Hospital screening cohort with radiologist BI-RADS assessment performed with and without commercial AI support, compared by AUC and sensitivity with 95% confidence intervals."
keyResults:
  - "AI-assisted read: AUC 75.2% (95% CI 75.0–75.5), sensitivity 61.5% (95% CI 60.8–61.6)."
  - "Unassisted read: AUC 73.2% (95% CI 72.9–73.2), sensitivity 57.5% (95% CI 57.2–58.1)."
  - "Study base of 1,119 eligible patients, Hanoi Medical University Hospital, November 2022 to April 2023."
  - "The exhibit concludes that AI assistance increased screening sensitivity in this cohort."
relevance: "Method papers are usually validated on public datasets. This is the same question asked inside a real screening workflow, in a population where the underlying problem — dense tissue masking lesions — is most acute."
limitation: "Retrospective single-centre design published as a conference e-poster rather than a full peer-reviewed article; it is not a prospective randomized comparison."
